Primary references

  • Generative Modelling With Inverse Heat Dissipation (Rissanen, Heinonen, Solin, ICLR 2023)

    Heat-equation forward process, noise sigma, reverse model, delta/sigma, K, sigma_B^2 = 2t, spectral statements; noise-free targets of Algorithm 1, the infinite-plane qualifier and the coarse-to-fine statement (read in the ar5iv HTML rendering)

  • Lazy Diffusion: Mitigating spectral collapse in generative diffusion-based stable autoregressive emulation of turbulent flows (Sambamurthy, Chattopadhyay, 2025)

    Forward DDPM path; Eq. 8 SNR; power-law schedule; lazy distillation loss and Section 3.2.4 sampling step; tau*=0.5; exponent sweep; spectral errors; systems studied

  • Lienen et al., From Zero to Turbulence: Generative Modeling for 3D Flow Simulation (ICLR 2024)

    2.2 TB dataset, grid, run count, ergodicity argument (via course chapter 5)

  • Solution to Exercise D.1, S. B. Pope, Turbulent Flows (Cambridge University Press, 2000), prepared by K. S. Kusumanchi, on the book's website

    Source note: the solution cites the book's Eqs. (D.1)-(D.2) with 1/(2 pi) on the forward transform and no prefactor on the inverse. Secondary and one-dimensional; the book's pages and its three-dimensional spectrum tensor were not read. No result of the chapter depends on it.

  • Chen, Rubanova, Bettencourt, Duvenaud, Neural Ordinary Differential Equations, arXiv 1806.07366 (2018, v6 read)

    Theorem 1 (Instantaneous Change of Variables), Section 4, eq. (8), PDF p. 4, and its Lipschitz hypothesis

  • Grathwohl, Chen, Bettencourt, Sutskever, Duvenaud, FFJORD, arXiv 1810.01367 (2018, v3 read; header 'Under review as a conference paper at ICLR 2019')

    eq. (3) total log-density change; eq. (4) backward likelihood solve, whose printed sign is opposite to eq. (3); Lipschitz hypothesis on f and its first derivatives, PDF p. 3; Section 3.1 trace cost, Hutchinson estimator eqs. (7)-(8)

  • Ambrosio, Crippa, Continuity equations and ODE flows with non-smooth velocity, lecture notes, Edinburgh, April 15-16, 2013 (repository copy read)

    uniqueness of weak solutions under (H): Remark 7, PDF p. 8; Theorem 9, PDF p. 9; Theorem 12, PDF p. 11

  • Teschl, Ordinary Differential Equations and Dynamical Systems, AMS (author's preliminary version read)

    method of Lemma 2.0: proof of Theorem 2.10, case k=1, PDF pp. 57-58, whose statement assumes joint C^k; second-order regularity quoted in Section 2.6: Theorems 2.10 and 6.1, PDF pp. 57 and 200

  • JHTDB forced isotropic turbulence data set (extended), README

    nu, DNS and storage time steps, 5028 frames over 0-10.056, about five large-eddy turnover times, k=0.705, eps=0.103, u'=0.686, Re_lambda=418, eta=0.00280, L=1.364, T_L=1.99, 2sqrt2/3 truncation, finite-difference divergence note

  • An approximate deconvolution procedure for large-eddy simulation (Stolz and Adams, Phys. Fluids 11(7), 1699-1701, 1999)

    Approximate deconvolution by truncated series expansion of the inverse filter operator (abstract only; operator details not verified)

  • generative-turbulence (TurbDiff released code)

    turbdiff/models/metrics.py: W2,TKE and W2,R are nested W2 distances; turbdiff/models/ddpm.py: scalar SNR 1e3 to 1e-5, log-linear

  • LazyDiffusion (released code, commit c004897, 2026-03-14)

    Target is the increment in both systems; the repository holds score training and a 1000-step reverse-SDE rollout, and no lazy retraining or one-step sampler; GulfOfMexico/losses.py holds an unimplemented note on cold/lazy diffusion at T = 1

  • JHTDB forced isotropic turbulence web page

    Re_lambda about 433, 5028 frames, storage every 10 DNS steps

  • Ohana et al., The Well: a Large-Scale Collection of Diverse Physics Simulations for Machine Learning

    existence of The Well collection; MHD_64 size and physics were measured for this course

Independent synthesis course built from the author's NPTEL and MIT study editions and primary research papers. Source and review limitations are recorded separately.
